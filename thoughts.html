How did humans learn to make rice?

May 15, 2026Question

I was eating rice at dinner and suddenly thought: who figured this out? Someone had to first find a wild grass, notice the grains were edible, figure out you need to boil them, and then decide to plant more of it.

Rice was first domesticated about 7,000–9,000 years ago in China's Yangtze River valley. But what I really wonder is — what was the first moment? Did a person just chew a raw rice grain and think "this could be something"?

Early humans must have made thousands of mistakes we'll never know about. Failed experiments. Inedible batches. I find that weirdly moving — all this anonymous trial and error, thousands of years ago, so we could have dinner.

How does the Earth rotate — and why doesn't it slow down?

May 8, 2026Question

It does slow down. Very slightly. The Moon's gravity creates tidal friction that is gradually lengthening our day — by about 1.4 milliseconds per century. When dinosaurs existed, a day was about 23 hours.

And there's nothing "pushing" the Earth — it's been rotating since it formed from a spinning disk of gas and dust 4.5 billion years ago. In space, with no friction to stop it, it just keeps going.

Will Earth ever stop rotating entirely? Apparently yes — in about 50 billion years. By then the Sun will have died anyway, so.

We live inside light

Apr 30, 2026Thought

Everything I see is light that left an object and reached my eye. The sun I see is 8 minutes old. Stars are thousands of years old. When I look at the night sky, I'm looking at history.

We're not just observing the universe. We're reading its past, constantly.
